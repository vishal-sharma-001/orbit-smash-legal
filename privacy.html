Orbit Smash privacy policy

Last updated: 26 November 2026

Orbit Smash ("the game") is made by an independent developer. This policy explains what the game collects and why. It describes the current version of the game; if that changes, this page changes first.

What the game collects

Nothing about you directly. The game has no accounts and no sign-in. It does not ask for your name, email, phone number, contacts, photos, location or microphone.

Your progress stays on your phone. Stars, coins, upgrades and settings are saved only on your device. Uninstalling the game deletes them. We cannot see them.

Ads (Google AdMob). The game shows optional rewarded ads: you choose to watch one to get a reward. To show and measure ads, Google AdMob may collect: - your device's advertising ID - your IP address - device and app information (model, OS version, language) - how you interacted with the ad

Google's use of this data is described at https://policies.google.com/technologies/ads and in Google's privacy policy at https://policies.google.com/privacy.

You can reset or delete your advertising ID, or opt out of personalised ads, in your phone's settings (Settings > Google > Ads, or Settings > Privacy > Ads).

No analytics and no crash reporting are sent by this version.

Children

The game is made for a general audience of teenagers and up, and its ads are limited to content rated for general audiences. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has provided personal information, contact us and we will help remove it.

Sharing

We do not sell or share personal information. The only third party that receives data is Google AdMob, for the ads described above.

Security

All ad traffic uses encrypted connections (HTTPS).

Your choices

  • Do not watch rewarded ads (they are always optional).
  • Reset your advertising ID or turn off ad personalisation in your phone's settings.
  • Uninstall the game to delete everything it stored on your phone.

Contact

Questions or requests: open an issue at https://github.com/vishal-sharma-001/orbit-smash-legal/issues (we read every one).
